# Decoded: What Your SIP Confirmation Email Isn't Telling You

*A "Decoded" piece from Abundance Financial Services · Atin Kumar Agrawal, ARN-251838*

Every month, a small transactional email lands in your inbox: units allotted, NAV, folio number, done. It's accurate, it's compliant, and it's telling you almost nothing about whether your SIP is actually working.

## What the email actually shows you

```
Dear Investor,
Your SIP instalment of Rs. 5,000.00 has been processed.
Units Allotted: 41.267
NAV: 121.1420
Folio No: 123456789
```

That's the entire picture, by design — it's a transaction receipt, not a portfolio review. It confirms the mechanics worked. It says nothing about the mechanics *mattering*.

## Three things missing from every SIP confirmation

**Your actual return (XIRR), not the fund's return.** A fund's published "3-year return" describes a lumpsum invested three years ago in one shot. Your SIP put in money on a different date every month, at a different NAV every time — your real, personal return is a completely different number, and no confirmation email ever shows it to you. Two people in the exact same fund, started six months apart, can have meaningfully different XIRRs today.

**Whether the amount still matches the goal.** A ₹5,000 SIP set up in 2019 to fund a goal was sized against 2019 prices and a 2019 income. If neither the goal's cost nor your income has been revisited since, the SIP amount is running on autopilot toward a target it was never updated to actually hit — a step-up that never happened is invisible in every single monthly confirmation, forever, until you go looking for it.

**How this instalment fits your overall allocation.** One confirmation email only ever describes one fund. If you're running SIPs across six schemes, no single email — and often no single app — shows you what the combined result actually looks like: are you accidentally 70% concentrated in one category because six "different" fund names all quietly hold the same large-cap stocks?

## Why this is easy to miss for years

None of this is hidden. It's just that a monthly confirmation is designed to answer one narrow question — *did this transaction go through* — and investors quietly start treating "the SIP is running" as equivalent to "the plan is on track." Those are two different facts, and the gap between them is exactly where SIPs that looked fine every single month for years turn out to have drifted well off the original goal by the time anyone checks.
