# Decoded: The Exit Load Fine Print That Quietly Costs Investors Crores a Year

*A "Decoded" piece from Abundance Financial Services · Atin Kumar Agrawal, ARN-251838*

Exit load is one line in a factsheet, easy to skim past, and one of the most common ways investors accidentally hand back part of their own returns — not because anyone hid anything, but because almost nobody checks the date before hitting redeem.

## What it actually is

An exit load is a small fee — typically around 1%, sometimes structured in tiers — charged when you redeem units before a specified holding period, usually somewhere between 15 days and a year depending on the scheme. It isn't a penalty in the punitive sense; it exists to discourage short-term in-and-out trading that would otherwise force the fund to keep excess cash on hand and hurt the returns of everyone who stays invested for the long haul. In principle, it protects long-term investors from short-term ones.

## Where people actually lose money on this

The mistake is almost never "I didn't know exit loads exist." It's far more specific than that:

- **Redeeming one or two days before the exit-load window closes**, because nobody checked the exact purchase date of that specific lot before redeeming.
- **Forgetting that every SIP instalment has its own individual holding-period clock.** A SIP running for three years still has last month's instalment sitting well inside the exit-load window — redeeming the whole scheme at once can trigger a load on the newest units even though the oldest ones cleared the window years ago.
- **Switching between plans or options** (Regular to Direct, Growth to IDCW) without realising a switch is treated as a redemption-plus-purchase for exit-load purposes on some schemes.

None of these are exotic mistakes. They're the default outcome of not checking one date before clicking one button — and multiplied across the millions of redemptions processed every month, that one-line fee in the fine print adds up to a genuinely large, entirely avoidable number nationally.

## How to actually check it

The exit-load clause is usually written as something like *"1% if redeemed within 365 days of allotment"* — and the operative word is *allotment*, not purchase intent, not the day you decided to sell. For SIPs, that clock resets with every single instalment. There's no shortcut here beyond actually looking it up before you redeem, for the specific scheme, at the specific redemption date you're planning.

## The honest bottom line

A 1% exit load sounds trivial next to years of compounding — and on a genuinely long-term holding, it usually is. The real cost isn't the fee itself; it's redeeming without knowing it applies, being surprised by a smaller-than-expected payout, and sometimes making a worse decision (holding on to a fund you'd rather have exited, purely to dodge a fee that a two-minute check could have flagged in advance).
